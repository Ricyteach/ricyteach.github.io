Buried Structure Analysis: CANDE and Soil-Structure Interaction

Buried flexible structures behave differently from above-ground members in ways that simplified design methods often can't capture. The soil surrounding a buried pipe isn't just a load source, it's also structural support, and the relationship between the two changes with installation conditions, backfill stiffness, compaction level, groundwater depth, and time. Getting the analysis right matters, especially for large-diameter pipe, non-standard installations, and rehabilitation projects where the structural condition of the host structure is uncertain.

What CANDE FEA Adds to Buried Structure Design

CANDE (Culvert ANalysis and DEsign) is a finite element program developed specifically for buried structure analysis under the AASHTO LRFD framework. Unlike the simplified Iowa Formula or closed-form AASHTO methods, CANDE models the soil and pipe as a coupled system, using nonlinear soil constitutive models to track how stiffness and load distribution change with stress. The output includes combined-strain safety factors, global and local buckling resistance, and deflection, computed for the actual installation geometry rather than a conservative closed-form envelope.

I reach for CANDE when simplified methods produce overly conservative or ambiguous results: non-standard embedment depths, unusual backfill materials, multi-stage installation sequences, or situations where a client needs to demonstrate actual performance rather than code-minimum compliance. The CANDE model is also the right tool when the installation deviates from the standard embedment conditions assumed in AASHTO Table 12.12.3.4-1.

Flexible Pipe and Culverts

My buried pipe work covers HDPE and profile-wall polyethylene, steel-reinforced polyethylene (SRPE), corrugated metal pipe, and reinforced concrete pipe, analyzed under AASHTO LRFD Section 12. Key limit states include wall compression strain, global and local buckling, the combined-strain interaction formula, and short-term versus long-term deflection under creep.

Anti-flotation and buoyancy analysis is part of most buried structure projects, including grout-uplift checks for relining pipe installations where the annular space grout creates an additional uplift load during placement. The installation stage often governs design in ways the final-condition analysis misses.

Culvert Relining and Rehabilitation

Relining an aging culvert is structurally distinct from designing a new pipe, because the liner must carry all load in the fully deteriorated condition with zero credit for the host structure. This changes the analysis substantially. I've worked with close-fit liners under ASCE MOP 145, fiberglass pipe liner design under AWWA M45 constrained buckling provisions, and custom equivalent-circular cross-checks for proprietary liners whose cross-section geometry doesn't fit a standard code category.

This is detail-oriented work, and the details matter: live-load designation, load factor framework (AASHTO LRFD versus working stress), ring thrust versus bending interaction, the distinction between constrained and unconstrained buckling modes, and how to handle the transition from a circular to an arch geometry in an equivalent-circular check. I document these decisions explicitly so the review path is traceable.

Precast Concrete Vaults, Boxes, and Manholes

Precast concrete buried structures have their own structural considerations that differ from cast-in-place work. Divider walls loaded by saturated media on one side and a dry or empty interior on the other are a common governing load case that's easy to overlook. Crack control requirements under ACI 318 Section 24.3 often govern design before yield strength does, particularly for walls in contact with soil or water. Anti-flotation analysis has to be checked under both water-only and saturated-backfill conditions, and the two cases don't necessarily bound each other.

I check precast vaults and manholes against ACI 318, AASHTO LRFD, and ASTM C890, with clearly documented load cases and code basis so any reviewing engineer can follow the analysis without guessing at assumptions.
