Structural Engineering: Steel, Cold-Formed, and Review Services

My structural practice covers a wide range of materials and project types, from individual member design and connection checks to full independent peer review of structural packages. The common thread is that I do the engineering work directly: I run the analysis, review the output, and seal what I've actually verified. No hand-offs, no junior staff doing the calculations while a senior PE signs the cover page.

Hot-Rolled Structural Steel

Member design for axial, flexural, shear, and combined loading under AISC 360. Connection design including bolted and welded joints, base plate sizing, and anchorage to concrete. Lateral-torsional buckling evaluation, bracing design, and cantilever column system design where the response modification factor selection actually matters.

I also do independent SAP2000 cross-checks of pre-engineered structures, which is where the gap between the original analysis and what the structure actually does tends to surface. Missing seismic analyses, incorrect R factors for cantilever column systems, and load paths that don't close through the diaphragm as assumed are findings from actual peer review work, not hypothetical risk items. See the AI + SAP2000 page for more on independent structural verification.

Typical project types include canopies and shelters, sign and display structures, equipment platforms, sports facility structures, and industrial support framing.

Cold-Formed Steel and Aluminum

Cold-formed steel and aluminum member design using both the Direct Strength Method (DSM) and the effective width method. Local, distortional, and flexural-torsional buckling modes under AISI S100, including the 2024 edition. Combined loading checks for members carrying axial and flexural demand simultaneously. I work with ICC-ES evaluation reports for pre-qualified section properties when the member geometry matches a listed product.

I've developed transparent, engineer-reviewable calculation frameworks for CFS hat sections and racking members that reference the code equations step by step, making it straightforward for a second engineer to follow and verify the design without needing to reverse-engineer the calculation structure. The same methodology applies to aluminum members under the Aluminum Design Manual.

Reinforced Concrete

Reinforced concrete design for foundations, walls, slabs, drilled shafts, and precast structures under ACI 318-19. Flexure, shear, and axial checks including crack control under Section 24.3 and minimum reinforcement provisions. Drilled shaft reinforcement including the ACI size-effect provisions on shear (the lambda-s factor) and confinement design. Anchorage and embedment under ACI 318 Chapter 17, including cast-in and post-installed anchor design for equipment and structural connections.

Plan Review and PE Stamping

I review and seal structural design packages produced by other engineers, with a documented cover report that records exactly what I reviewed, what I checked independently, what I relied on the designer for, and any qualifications or limitations on the review scope. That documentation practice exists because a PE seal without a clear review record is ambiguous in the event of a dispute, and I'd rather be specific about what I did and didn't do.

Clear discipline boundaries apply: I stamp structural work within my competence and decline requests outside it. If a package has structural and architectural components and only the structural work has been engineered, I'll seal the structural drawings and say so explicitly.

Independent Peer Review

Structural peer review with a focus on finding real issues. The goal is a second opinion that actually functions as one, not a list of formatting comments and requests for clarification. I'm comfortable identifying genuine deficiencies (missing seismic analyses, incorrect code editions, mislabeled load cases, inconsistent load factor frameworks) and distinguishing them from items that are conservative or unconventional but defensible. I document both categories clearly so the designer knows what needs to change and what's a judgment call.
