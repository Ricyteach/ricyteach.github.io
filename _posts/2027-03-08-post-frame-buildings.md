Pole barns, post frame garages, and post frame houses, often called barndominiums, are common across Ohio, and I prepare drawings for them for contractors and owners. They are economical because they use few, widely spaced members and a thin steel skin. The structural system works differently from a conventionally framed house, and the differences explain much of what a building department asks for and many of the problems in post frame designs that were not engineered.

## How a post frame building is put together

The primary members are posts, either solid timber or columns laminated from several pieces of lumber, set along each side wall at a spacing much wider than house wall studs. The posts are either embedded in the ground or fastened with steel brackets to concrete piers or a concrete wall. A roof truss sits on each pair of posts, or on a header spanning between posts.

Purlins run across the trusses and support the roof panels, and girts run horizontally across the posts and support the wall panels. The panels are usually corrugated steel, screwed to the purlins and girts.

## How it resists wind

Wind on a long side wall pushes on the panels. The panels span between girts, the girts span between posts, and each post acts as a vertical beam with a support at the ground and a second support at the eave.

The support at the eave comes from the roof. A steel roof skin screwed to the purlins at closely spaced fasteners is stiff in its own plane and acts as a diaphragm: a deep, thin horizontal beam that collects the push from the tops of all the side wall posts and delivers it to the end walls. The end walls, also skinned with steel, act as shear walls and take that force down to their foundations.

Without the diaphragm, each post would resist the wind alone as a cantilever from the ground, which requires much larger posts and deeper embedment. With the diaphragm working, part of the load at the eave goes to the end walls. The engineering practices for post frame construction published by the American Society of Agricultural and Biological Engineers describe how to calculate that sharing between the posts and the diaphragm.

## Why the embedded post is a lateral design problem

An embedded post delivers the weight of the roof to a concrete footing pad at the bottom of the hole, and bearing on that pad is a straightforward calculation. Wind uplift is resisted by concrete collars or blocks fastened near the bottom of the post, which engage the weight of soil above them.

The demanding check is lateral: the post resists the wind that reaches its base by pushing sideways against the soil along its embedded length, and the depth of embedment needed for that is usually what sets the hole depth. That is the same problem the article [How a Pile Resists a Sideways Shove](/articles/laterally-loaded-piles/) describes for sign and pole foundations, applied to a short, stiff post. A concrete floor slab poured against the posts can provide restraint at grade, and the drawings have to state whether the design relies on it. Most brackets transfer little bending to the pier, so a post on a bracket depends on the diaphragm for nearly all of its lateral support.

## Where designs that were not engineered go wrong

In designs that were not engineered, the diaphragm is usually the part that is wrong, because nothing about it looks structural. It depends on the panels, the fastener type, and the fastener spacing, and a builder can change any of them without realizing the structure changed.

Common problems are a roof fastener pattern too sparse to develop diaphragm action, large overhead doors that remove most of an end wall the diaphragm delivers its load to, and a building so long that the diaphragm deflects too much to help the middle posts. Open sides change the wind loads themselves. A machine shed open along one wall can be classified as a partially enclosed, partially open, or open building under the wind provisions of ASCE 7, the loading standard adopted by most building codes, and the pressures differ from those on an enclosed building, as the article [Wind on Structures That Are Mostly Air](/articles/wind-open-structures/) explains.

Snow load is the other common source of error. Snow drifts against a taller adjacent roof and slides off steel roofs onto lower ones, adding load to purlins and trusses sized for a uniform depth.

## What changes when it becomes a residence

Building codes place agricultural buildings in a low risk category, because few people occupy them, and that category permits lower design wind and snow loads. Some states, Ohio among them, exempt many agricultural buildings from the building code, and a residence does not qualify for that exemption. A post frame building used as a house is designed as a house. The design wind and snow loads increase, and the building department reviews it as a residence.

Drywall ceilings and walls crack under deflections that steel panels tolerate, so the posts, trusses, and diaphragm are designed to tighter deflection limits. Ceilings, insulation, hung equipment, and a loft add load to the trusses and posts. A house also needs frost-protected foundations, moisture control under a steel roof, and the fire, egress, and energy provisions of any dwelling, which are matters for the designer of the house and the building department.

## What a building department typically wants

A permit submission for an engineered post frame building commonly includes drawings showing the design loads (wind speed, exposure category, risk category, ground snow load, and the assumed soil bearing pressure), a foundation plan with post embedment, footing pads, and uplift resistance, framing plans and sections showing posts, girts, purlins, and bracing, connection details at the truss to post, purlin to truss, and girt to post connections, the fastener schedule for the roof and wall panels where the diaphragm depends on it, and truss design drawings from the truss manufacturer. Exposure category describes how open the surrounding terrain is to the wind, and risk category classifies the building by the hazard its failure would pose to people.
