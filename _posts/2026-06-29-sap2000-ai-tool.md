*And what it does, and does not, mean for your project.*

Every structural engineer who uses SAP2000 knows the part of the job that is not really engineering. Before you can analyze anything, you have to build the model: nodes, frames, sections, supports, load patterns, load cases, combinations. On a routine job that setup can take hours, and all of those hours go to data entry.

A SAP2000 model is, underneath the interface, structured data. The geometry, the section properties, the loads, all of it can be expressed as a text file that the program reads back in. The time goes into translating engineering intent into the exact format the software expects. That is a translation problem, and translation is something modern artificial intelligence is genuinely good at.

So I built a tool that does the translating. I describe a structure in plain language, and the artificial intelligence assembles the corresponding SAP2000 input file. That sentence makes it sound easy. It was not.

## The part most people miss

A model like Claude is very good at producing structured text. But it does not know SAP2000's input format. None of that format lives in its general knowledge, and the format itself is unforgiving: a dense text specification full of ordering rules, unit conventions, escaping behavior, and quirks that break silently when you get them slightly wrong. You cannot simply ask a language model to "write me a SAP2000 file" and trust what comes back. It will produce something that looks correct and is wrong in ways that invalidate the analysis.

Getting it to work meant teaching the model the entire format, through a long iterative process: build, test against real models, find exactly where it breaks, correct, and repeat, over and over, across a great deal of time and computing expense. What you end up with is called a skill, a hard-won body of encoded knowledge that lets the model produce input the program will actually accept and that means what I intended it to mean. None of that knowledge was quick to acquire, and none of it is the sort of thing that gets finished in a weekend.

What this really demands is fluency in two domains at the same time. You have to know the structural software deeply enough to catch when its output is subtly wrong, because these systems will confidently produce subtly wrong things, and you have to understand how they behave and fail well enough to make them reliable. There are plenty of structural engineers. There are plenty of people who can prompt a language model. The overlap, people who can do both well enough to build something a licensed engineer would be willing to take responsibility for, is very small. That overlap is the entire reason this practice exists.

## Why you can trust a model built this way

The sensible question any client asks is this: if a machine built it, why should I trust it? The answer is that the tool never gets the final word and never makes an engineering decision. Every model is round-trip validated, generated, read back, and checked against what I actually intended, and then it goes through the same review any model of mine would. A licensed professional engineer verifies it and takes responsibility for it. The tool does the assembly. The engineering stays human. That division is deliberate, and it is the whole design.

## What it actually buys you

Speed, without a quality penalty. Work that used to take days, parametric studies, design options, product calculators that need the same model rebuilt a hundred different ways, now finishes in hours. For repetitive and parametric problems especially, the difference is large, and it appears in both your schedule and your fee.

It does not do everything, and I would be wary of anyone who told you their version did. It does not do the conceptual design, the part where you decide what the structure should be. It does not replace an engineer's assessment of whether an answer is even reasonable. There are problems where it offers no advantage at all, and on those I build the model by hand like everyone else. The tool is excellent at the tedious, well-defined assembly that used to consume my time, and it has no role in the parts that genuinely require an engineer. That boundary is exactly what makes the rest of it trustworthy.
