A home inspection report, an insurance adjuster's note, or a notice from a city building department often contains some version of the same sentence: have this condition evaluated by a licensed structural engineer. For a buyer in a contingency period, a seller with a listing date, an agent, or an investor rehabilitating a vacant building, that sentence raises practical questions. This article answers them in the order they usually come up. I am based in Dayton and make site visits in the Dayton, Springfield, and Columbus areas, and inspection findings, insurance questions, and city orders have prompted several recent inquiries I have received.

## What the sentence means

A home inspector is a generalist. In a few hours the inspection covers the roof, the plumbing, the electrical and heating systems, the finishes, and the structure, and its purpose is to identify conditions. When the inspector sees a crack, a bowed wall, a sagging floor, or damaged framing and cannot tell from a visual inspection whether the condition is structural, the usual practice is to recommend evaluation by a specialist.

The sentence means the inspector found something a home inspection is not designed to resolve. Whether the condition is structural, what caused it, and whether it needs repair are the questions the engineer answers. Sometimes the answer is that the condition is cosmetic, or that it records movement that stopped years ago.

Adjusters and city inspectors use the same sentence for related reasons. The adjuster needs an opinion on the cause and extent of damage, and the city needs a licensed professional's written statement before it allows repairs or occupancy.

## Whether photographs can answer the question

Sometimes they can. Photographs can show that a condition is plainly minor, such as a hairline shrinkage crack in a floor slab, or plainly serious. Photographs usually show whether a site visit is needed and what it would cover.

A written opinion on a structural condition generally requires a visit. The measurements that decide the question, and the framing and foundation below the condition, are seldom visible in a photograph. Where I do write a letter from photographs alone, the letter states that basis and the limits that come with it.

## How the scope is set

An inspection report frequently lists several items that could be structural: a crack in the foundation wall, a ridge line that sags, a joist cut through for a drain line. The scope can be a single item or every structural item in the report. I agree on the scope in writing before the visit. If the agreed scope is one item, the letter addresses that item and states that the other items were outside the scope.

## What the deliverable is

The deliverable is a stamped findings letter. It describes the structure as I found it, records the measurements and photographs, gives my opinion on whether the condition is structural, what caused it, and whether it is stable or progressing, and lists recommendations in order of urgency along with the limitations of the evaluation. A letter on a bowing basement wall, for example, records the measurements described in the article [Bowing Basement Walls: Causes, Measurement, and Repair](/articles/bowing-basement-walls/), including the inward displacement at several heights and the crack widths.

Where a repair is needed, the letter states what the repair must accomplish. A designed repair detail, drawn for a contractor to build from or for a permit application, is a separate item of scope that can be added when it is needed.

## Timing and the contingency period

Contingency periods are short, and a visit depends on access. The seller has to allow it, utilities may be off in a vacant house, and stored belongings or finishes may cover the area in question. The practical steps are to send the report as soon as it arrives, state the deadline in the first message, and have the agents arrange access early.

## Who may rely on the letter

Reliance is the term for which parties are entitled to base their decisions on the letter, with the engineer responsible to them for its content. The letter is written for the client who engaged me, and it names that client.

A seller who obtains a letter before listing can share it with prospective buyers for their information. A buyer who reads it learns what was observed, what was measured, and what I concluded, which can answer the question before the buyer's own inspector raises it. The letter describes the building on the date of the visit. A buyer or a lender who wants an opinion addressed to them can arrange that by agreement before the work, or engage me separately.

## When the city requires the evaluation

Cities commonly require an engineer's evaluation during the rehabilitation of vacant property. An order lists the conditions the city observed and requires a structural engineer to evaluate them before it issues permits for the repairs or allows the building to be occupied.

In that case the letter is written for the building official as much as for the owner. It identifies which framing or foundation elements need repair and what each repair must accomplish, clearly enough that the official can check the completed work against it. A city order may also require other professionals, such as an architect for fire separation between units or licensed trades for mechanical and electrical work. Those are separate scopes, and my letter states which items of the order it addresses.

## What to send me

Send the report or notice itself, with the page that describes the condition and the inspector's photographs. Add your own photographs, including a wide view that shows where the condition is in the building, the address and approximate age of the building, the deadline you are working against, and the name of the person or company the letter is for. The site visit follows the method in the article [Reading an Existing Structure Before You Analyze It](/articles/condition-assessment/), and the scope, the report contents, and the limits of a visual evaluation are described on my [condition assessment page](/condition-assessment/).
