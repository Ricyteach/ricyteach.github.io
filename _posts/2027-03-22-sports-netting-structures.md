Barrier netting behind a baseball backstop, along a golf range, or at the end of a soccer field keeps balls inside the facility. Structurally, it is a row of tall poles with a light net hung between them. Nearly all of the load is wind on the net, plus the tension the net applies to the poles.

I have reviewed several sets of pole and drilled pier drawings for a netting fabricator. This article describes how these structures are designed and what a review of the drawings checks.

## Each pole is a cantilever

Each pole is fixed at the ground and free at the top, with no bracing between poles except the net and any cables. Each pole collects the wind on the width of net it supports. The wind force acts high above the ground, and the bending moment it produces grows with height, so the moment at the base of a tall pole is large compared with the force that causes it. That moment governs the pole section at the base, the base connection, and the foundation.

## How the net enters the wind calculation

The wind acts on the solid material in a net, meaning the twine and the knots, and the rest of the air passes through. The solidity ratio is the solid area divided by the gross area inside the outline of the net. A net with fine twine and a large mesh opening has a low solidity ratio, and a heavier net with smaller openings has a higher one.

ASCE 7, the loading standard published by the American Society of Civil Engineers and adopted by most building codes, does not name sports netting. Netting is usually designed with its provisions for open structures, which give a force coefficient that depends on solidity, using the edition adopted by the jurisdiction where the facility is built. Those provisions are described in the article [Wind on Structures That Are Mostly Air](/articles/wind-open-structures/).

Solidity is a property of the specific net, and it changes over a net's life. A net with debris caught in it, ice on it, or a windscreen or banner fastened to it is more solid than the same net clean. If the owner can attach a windscreen, the structure has to be designed for that condition, or the drawings have to prohibit it.

## Net tension

A net hung with sag needs some tension to keep its shape, and wind on the net pushes it into a deeper curve, which increases the tension. The poles at the ends of a run and at corners resist that tension in one direction, without a matching net on the other side to balance it, so they usually need a larger section, a guy cable, or a larger foundation than the interior poles. Interior poles receive net tension from both sides, which largely cancels along the line of the net, while they still bend under the wind on the net.

## Net raised, lowered, or removed

Many systems let the owner lower the net or remove it for the off season, which creates more than one design condition. With the net raised, the poles see the full wind on the net. With the net lowered or removed, the poles see wind on the bare pole, which is much smaller, but the poles may stand through the storm season in that condition. The loading standard does not reduce the design wind for an operating procedure, so the poles are normally designed for the net raised in the full design wind. A design that relies on lowering the net has to state that basis on the drawings and needs the building official's acceptance.

## Pole design: bending with some axial load

A netting pole is designed for combined bending and axial load. The bending comes from wind on the net and on the pole itself. The axial load comes from the weight of the pole, the net, and any hardware, and from the vertical component of guy cable tension where cables are used. For a tall slender pole, the axial load can add to the bending through the deflection of the pole, and the design accounts for that effect. Steel pipe and tube poles are designed under the Specification for Structural Steel Buildings of the American Institute of Steel Construction, and the base connection, whether a base plate with anchor rods or a pole embedded directly in concrete, is checked for the same moment.

A pole that is strong enough can still sway a great deal at the top, which can stress the net attachments and concern the people who see it.

## The drilled pier is a lateral load problem

Netting poles are usually founded on drilled piers, which are cylinders of reinforced concrete cast in a drilled hole. The pier receives a small vertical load and a large overturning moment. That is a lateral load problem, the one described in the article [How a Pile Resists a Sideways Shove](/articles/laterally-loaded-piles/), and the depth and diameter of the pier are set by the soil resistance along its length and by how far the top may move. The soil parameters come from a geotechnical report where one exists, and where none exists, from the presumptive soil values in the building code, stated on the drawings.

## What a review of the pole and pier drawings checks

A review of a fabricator's drawings follows the order described in the article [What a Shop Drawing Review Actually Checks](/articles/shop-drawing-review/), beginning with the loads. For netting, that means:

- The edition of ASCE 7 and the wind speed and exposure for the site.
- The solidity used for the net, and whether it matches the net actually specified.
- Whether a windscreen, banner, or similar attachment is allowed, and whether the design includes it.
- The net tension at end and corner poles, and how it is resisted.
- The net raised and lowered conditions, and which one the design relies on.
- Pole capacity at the base for combined bending and axial load, and top deflection.
- The base connection or embedment detail.
- The drilled pier depth and diameter, the reinforcement, and the soil parameters assumed.
- Internal consistency between the pole schedule, the elevations, and the foundation details.

Findings are separated into items that need resolution before work proceeds and comments that do not affect adequacy. When the required items are resolved, a stamped letter of review follows. The scope and what to send are on my [shop drawing review page](/shop-drawing-review/).
